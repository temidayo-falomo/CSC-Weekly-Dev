## Google Forms setup

1. Open https://script.google.com/ and create a new project in your own Google account.
2. Copy `create-google-form.gs` into the script editor, save, choose `createIroQuestionnaire`, and run it. Review Google's authorization request. It creates one Form and one linked response spreadsheet; it sends no invitations.
3. Open the execution log for the edit URL, respondent URL and response spreadsheet URL. Running again reuses the same saved Form instead of duplicating it.
4. Preview the Form. Verify declining consent exits the questionnaire and all three role options are present. Keep automatic email collection disabled. Check your account's responder-access restrictions and publish the Form if required by its UI.
5. Copy the actual respondent URL here and into the repository guide. Share it with consenting students and indigenes using a channel you are authorized to use.

## Introduction and consent

This voluntary academic survey takes about 3-5 minutes. It asks how students and indigenes report community issues. Do not provide your name, matric number, phone number, private addresses, names of accused people or details of sensitive incidents. You may skip optional questions or exit without submitting. Submitted responses are stored in the researcher's Google account and may be reviewed for course assessment. The researcher should agree a retention period and communicate it before distribution. This is not an emergency or complaints reporting channel.

## Analysis plan - after genuine responses are received

Count respondents by role and community; show counts and percentages with denominators. Keep the Both group distinct to avoid double counting. Summarize current channels, satisfaction and barriers; exclude Never used them from any satisfaction-only average. Checkbox percentages may exceed 100%; flag contradictory selections such as None alongside an issue type. Compare students and indigenes cautiously, especially with small samples. Summarize free-text themes after removing personal details. Report sample size, dates, distribution channels and convenience-sampling limitations. Use the evidence to revise the Ishikawa hypotheses and SRS; do not fabricate responses.
